**Performative complicity.
Gendered Consumption, the Pink Tax, and the Social Construction of Femininity**

Investigated the gap between consumer awareness and purchasing behavior in gendered markets (personal care and children's toys) in Bucharest. Combining an empirical retail price comparison (identifying 10%–35% markups on functionally identical products) with 10 semi-structured qualitative interviews, this research introduces the concept of performative complicity—explaining how identity work, social friction, and brand positioning keep consumers loyal to segmented products even when they recognize the markup.

A mixed-methods sociological and market research study investigating how gender-based market segmentation and differential pricing (the "pink tax") shape everyday purchasing decisions.   

**Research Question**: Why do consumers who recognize that gendered marketing and price premiums are arbitrary continue to purchase feminized products anyway?  
Methodology:Retail Price Audit: Comparative in-store pricing analysis across functionally identical personal care products and children's toys in Bucharest (documenting 10%–35% price premiums on feminized packaging).   Qualitative Research: 10 in-depth, semi-structured interviews (5 women without children, 5 mothers) analyzed via hybrid deductive/inductive thematic coding.   

**Key Finding** (Performative Complicity): Critical consumer awareness does not automatically lead to behavioral exit. Purchasing decisions are driven by habit, sensory pleasure, social norms, and identity signaling—making gendered pricing an effective commercial infrastructure rather than simple price gouging.
