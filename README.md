## Overview

This repository presents a mixed-methods sociological and market research study investigating the gap between consumer awareness and purchasing behavior in gender-segmented markets. Focusing on adult personal care products and children's toys in urban Romania, the research examines how commercial market segmentation and gender-based differential pricing (the **"pink tax"**) operate as an everyday infrastructure through which femininity is produced, performed, and made economically costly.

By combining an empirical retail price audit in Bucharest with in-depth qualitative interviewing, this study introduces the analytical concept of **performative complicity**—explaining why informed consumers continue purchasing marked-up feminized products even when they recognize the pricing strategy as arbitrary.

---

## Core Research Puzzle

> **Why do consumers who recognize that gendered marketing and price premiums are arbitrary continue to purchase feminized products anyway?**

While existing consumer research often treats the pink tax strictly as a pricing anomaly or assumes that consumer awareness leads to rational market exit, this study investigates the social, relational, and identity-based mechanisms that sustain consumer loyalty to gender-segmented goods.

---

## Methodology

The research employs a two-part empirical design conducted in Bucharest, Romania between February and April 2026:

### 1. Empirical Retail Price Audit
* **Location:** Jumbo Romania, Bucharest (March 2026)
* **Design:** Comparative in-store pricing analysis across 7 pairs of functionally identical products segmented into "men's/boys'" and "women's/girls'" lines.
* **Categories Audited:** Everyday personal care items (razors, antiperspirant deodorant, body lotion, shower gel) and children's toys (toy kitchen/workshop, science experiment kit, bicycle helmet).

### 2. Qualitative Interviewing & Thematic Analysis
* **Sample ($N = 10$):** Purposive sample of urban Romanian women aged 24–45 residing in Bucharest, divided into two analytical cohorts:
  * **Group A ($n = 5$):** Women without children (ages 24–35) — capturing adult identity work and everyday personal care routines.
  * **Group B ($n = 5$):** Mothers of at least one child under age 12 (ages 34–45) — capturing household purchasing trade-offs and intergenerational gender socialization in the toy market.
* **Data Collection & Analysis:** Semi-structured interviews (42–78 minutes each; 58-minute average), transcribed verbatim and analyzed using Braun & Clarke's (2006) six-stage thematic analysis with hybrid deductive and inductive coding. All participant data are anonymized.

## Key Empirical Findings

### Retail Price Differentials (10%–35% Markups)
In-store data collection documented consistent price premiums on feminized versions of functionally equivalent items:

| Product Category | Men's / Neutral Version | Women's / Girls' Version | Pink Tax Premium |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Disposable razor (5-pack)** | 9.99 RON | 13.49 RON | **+35%** |
| **Moisturising body lotion (400ml)** | 16.99 RON | 22.99 RON | **+35%** |
| **Shower gel (500ml)** | 10.49 RON | 13.99 RON | **+33%** |

### Analytical Contributions
1. **Performative Complicity:** Critical consumer awareness does not automatically produce behavioral exit. Consumers recognize the constructed nature of gendered marketing yet remain invested in feminized consumption due to sensory pleasure, habitual routines, identity signaling, and the social friction of non-conformity.
2. **Commercial Infrastructure over Price Gouging:** The pink tax operates less as an isolated pricing trick and more as a coordinated market segmentation system—combining packaging design, scent coding, and retail aisle segregation—that constructs femininity as a distinct consumption category.
3. **Class & Exit Options:** While middle-class urban consumers possess the financial margin and consumer literacy to selectively cross over into men's or unisex aisles, lower-income consumers absorb a proportionally heavier economic burden with fewer practical exit options.
4. **Intergenerational Socialization:** In the toy market, mothers actively mediate between commercial gender scripts and child-rearing—practicing forms of "consumer literacy" and counter-socialization (e.g., purchasing ungendered STEM/construction toys) while navigating peer pressure on their children.
